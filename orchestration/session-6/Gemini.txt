# 2. PROJECT DESCRIPTION

## 2.1 CBAM EU Regulatory Context
The European Union’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) entered its definitive enforcement phase on 1 January 2026, transitioning from the non-punitive reporting period of previous years. Under this current framework, a financial phase-in tariff of 2.5% is active for the fiscal year 2026. This tariff escalates linearly to achieve 100% full enforcement by 2034, systematically removing the free carbon allowances historically granted to European industries and equalizing carbon pricing for imports.

The regulatory scope strictly targets high-emission upstream sectors: iron, steel, aluminum, cement, and fertilizers. Driven by European Energy Exchange (EEX) spot market data, the reference price for CBAM certificates is established at €75.36 per ton of carbon dioxide equivalent (€75.36/tCO2). The hard deadline for the first mandatory purchase of these certificates by EU importers is set for February 2027, covering all embedded emissions generated during the 2026 production year.


```

+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                          CBAM TIMELINE ROADMAP                          |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2023 - 2025: Transitional Phase (Data Collection Only)                  |
| 2026: Definitive Phase Begins (2.5% Financial Phase-In Active)          |
| Feb 2027: First Hard Financial Settlement Deadline for 2026 Emissions   |
| 2027 - 2033: Linear Annual Escalation of Financial Exposure             |
| 2034: 100% Full Enforcement & Total Abolition of Free Allowances        |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+

```

For Indonesian manufacturers, the operational risk is indirect yet financially disruptive. If an exporter cannot provide third-party verified, actual embedded emissions data, EU customs authorities automatically apply the "Default Value" penalty—a metric calculated using the emission intensities of the highest-emitting producers within the EU or global supply chain. This arbitrary inflation of carbon footprint metrics results in severe financial penalties levied via certificate costs, completely eroding the thin operating margins of Southeast Asian products in the European single market.

## 2.2 Indonesia Export Landscape
Data from the Bureau of Statistics (BPS) and the Ministry of Trade of the Republic of Indonesia indicates that the nation’s total export exposure to the European Union stands at USD 21.5 billion across all sectors. The subset of industrial commodities directly vulnerable to immediate CBAM penalties—specifically iron, steel, and aluminum—represents a critical trade value of USD 757.7 million.

The Indonesian Iron and Steel Industry Association (IISIA) notes that outbound shipments to the EU constitute approximately 4% of Indonesia's total global steel export volume. Under the future 100% full enforcement paradigm, the total projected carbon certificate liability for the domestic steel supply chain climbs to an estimated €383 million, translating to a punitive overhead of €390 per ton of steel. 

Conversely, under the active 2026 phase-in period (2.5% tariff), the immediate localized financial exposure is calculated at €9.75 per ton of steel, representing a cumulative national market exposure of approximately €9.57 million. This transitional financial window establishes a critical timeline: manufacturers must deploy certified carbon data infrastructure before the February 2027 settlement deadline to prevent catastrophic contract terminations. This structural compliance pressure bears down directly upon 1,500 to 2,000 industrial manufacturing enterprises nationwide.

# 5. RECOMMENDED SOLUTION

## 5.1 Solution Overview
Emitra is a high-security, B2B data engineering platform constructed to automate the end-to-end extraction, calculation, and formal reporting of embedded emissions for industrial exporters. The platform acts as an automated data bridge. It aggregates raw, unstandardized operational logs from factory floors and transforms them into verified, schema-compliant data payloads ready for ingestion by the European Commission's CBAM Transitional Registry.

### Engineering Principle
In trade compliance environments, absolute reproducibility and auditability take priority over predictive artificial intelligence. Emitra implements a hybrid system design. It utilizes Machine Learning solely for the automation of high-friction, manual tasks—such as parsing unstandardized document layouts and identifying data anomalies—while utilizing a strictly deterministic, hard-coded computation engine for the calculation of final emissions. This ensures that every numerical value generated can be traced back to its raw material source, invoice, or meter reading.

## 5.2 System Architecture
The platform's technical architecture is divided into five specialized layers:

### Layer 1: Data Ingestion Pipeline
The ingestion layer accommodates four primary categories of unstandardized operational inputs via automated Secure File Transfer Protocol (SFTP) endpoints, direct application programming interface (API) calls, or secure client web dashboards:
*   **Scope 2 Data:** Commercial electricity invoices (PLN) and sub-meter consumption logs.
*   **Scope 1 Data:** Hydrocarbon fuel procurement invoices, natural gas flow data, and stationary combustion logs.
*   **Production Volume Data:** Mill sheets, shipping bills of lading, and export manifest data.
*   **Precursor/Material Composition Logs:** Bills of materials (BOM) specifying raw scrap steel or aluminum ingot tracking numbers.

### Layer 2: Document AI Engine
Unstructured inputs (such as image-only PDFs, scans, or smartphone photos from factory floors) enter an advanced machine learning extraction pipeline. The system utilizes a specialized multimodal sequence model (LayoutLM) that evaluates both text positions and visual layouts simultaneously. This enables the system to reliably locate, isolate, and digitize key value pairs (e.g., total kilowatt-hours consumed, metric tons of coke purchased, invoice date ranges) regardless of the document layout.

### Layer 3: Data Normalization & Validation Queue
Once digitized, the data is transformed into a standardized internal schema. This layer runs real-time statistical anomaly detection using an Isolation Forest algorithm to flags input data errors. If a fuel invoice exhibits a consumption metric that deviates significantly from historical trends, the transaction is routed to a specialized validation queue rather than entering the calculation pipeline.

### Layer 4: Deterministic Computation Engine
The computation engine relies entirely on deterministic code. It maps normalized activity data against the exact greenhouse gas (GHG) calculation methodologies specified by the European Commission. The core mathematical model evaluates specific embedded emissions through direct formulas:

$$E_{embedded} = \frac{DirEm + IndEm}{Prod_{Alg}}$$

Where:
*   $DirEm$ represents Scope 1 direct emissions generated from stationary combustion and industrial chemical reductions within the factory boundaries.
*   $IndEm$ represents Scope 2 indirect emissions calculated by applying the official grid emission factors of the Indonesian electrical grid to verified electricity consumption data.
*   $Prod_{Alg}$ represents the total metric tons of specific output material produced during the matching attribution period.

### Layer 5: Compliance Output & Serialization
Once validated, the data is serialized into the mandatory XML format required by the European Commission. Before delivery, the file undergoes an internal XML Schema Definition (XSD) validation to ensure it matches the syntax rules of the EU CBAM Registry, preventing system rejections at the customs border.

## 5.3 Technical Pipeline Matrix
The table below specifies how artificial intelligence and deterministic components are separated within the platform architecture:

| Technical Subsystem | Applied Computational Approach | Operational Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Document Structural Parsing** | Multimodal LayoutLM + Character Recognition | Digitizes varied, unstandardized local paper invoices and logs into structured tables. |
| **Commodity HS Code Classification** | Supervised Text Classification Model | Maps specific factory products to mandatory CBAM Combined Nomenclature (CN) codes. |
| **Consumption Anomaly Scoring** | Isolation Forest / Statistical Z-Score Filters | Prevents data corruption by automatically flagging outlier spikes caused by clerical errors. |
| **Emissions Calculation Core** | Deterministic, Rule-Based Logic Engine | Computes exact emissions values via immutable regulatory formulas to guarantee audit reproducibility. |
| **Schema Validation Validation** | Local XSD Compilation | Validates syntax structures against EU customs endpoints prior to final export. |

### Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Validation Core
To maintain thin support overheads while ensuring 100% accurate data inputs, Emitra features an integrated Human-in-the-Loop validation interface. When the Document AI engine encounters a degraded or handwritten document where the character recognition confidence score drops below 92%, the system pauses automated processing for that file. 

It generates a split-screen browser interface for the client's internal administrative staff: the raw document image is displayed on the left, with pre-filled editable form fields highlighting the low-confidence data on the right. This allows the client's own team to rapidly review and confirm data points. This design ensures that the data validation workload scales out to the user base rather than creating internal operational bottlenecks for Emitra's engineering team.

# 8. RISK ANALYSIS

## 8.1 Technical & Data Risks
*   **The Ingestion Data Quality Deficit:** Incomplete records, blurry smartphone photos, or handwritten logs from factory floors can cause data errors. *Mitigation:* The system uses integrated input filters and sets an automatic threshold flag. If data quality is poor, the system routes the file to the split-screen Human-in-the-Loop validation interface, ensuring the data is checked before it reaches the calculation core.
*   **Regulatory Schema Drift:** Sudden changes to the EU's reporting format can break software output compatibility. *Mitigation:* The system relies on a modular application architecture where all calculation rules and target schemas are stored in isolated configuration files, allowing updates to be deployed instantly without requiring rewrites of the core platform code.

## 8.2 Operational & Liability Risks
*   **Data Breach Vulnerabilities:** Industrial energy and production logs represent highly sensitive trade data. *Mitigation:* Emitra implements strict end-to-end encryption protocols alongside row-level multi-tenant database isolation, scheduling annual independent penetration tests to align with ISO/IEC 27001 data security standards.
*   **Financial Indemnification Demands:** Exporters may demand financial compensation if an unexpected system error leads to customs delays or penalties. *Mitigation:* The company's terms of service establish a clear legal boundary: the platform operates as a data processing tool, not a certified human verifier. Final liability for customs submissions remains with the client and their designated third-party auditing body, with Emitra's financial liability strictly capped at the subscription fees paid.

## 9.1 Phase 1: Domestic Consolidation (Years 1–2)
The immediate operational focus is to secure a dominant position within the Indonesian industrial market. By working closely with industrial estate operators in Cikarang, Karawang, and Cilegon, Emitra aims to onboard 100 domestic client sites by Year 3.

The target is to secure 320 active regional corporate clients by the end of Year 5, capturing 10% of the regional Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM).
