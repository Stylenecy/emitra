Indonesia stands at a critical juncture in its trade relationship
with the European Union, with approximately USD 19.28
billion in total exports to the EU in 2025, of which carbon-
intensive commodities are particularly iron, steel, and
aluminum account for USD 757.7 million. However, the
European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism
```
(CBAM), which entered its definitive phase on January 1,
```
2026, has fundamentally altered the competitive landscape
for Indonesian exporters by requiring EU importers to
purchase carbon certificates equivalent to the embedded
emissions of imported goods. Without verified actual
emission data, EU importers are forced to apply punitive
"default values" which the emission averages calculated from
the world's highest-emitting producers resulting in
significantly higher certificate costs that erode Indonesia's
price competitiveness. The Indonesian Iron and Steel Industry
```
Association (IISIA) estimates that full CBAM enforcement
```
could increase export costs for steel products by 5-20%, with
total certificate costs projected to reach approximately €383
million by 2034 for Indonesia's steel supply chain alone,
while EU importers face their first hard financial settlement
deadline in February 2027, creating an immediate mandate
for exporters to build emissions data infrastructure that most
Tier-2 and Tier-3 manufacturers currently lack.
