# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) marks a significant shift in international trade by requiring carbon emissions transparency for imported products. Indonesian exporters in carbon-intensive industries—particularly iron, steel, and aluminum—are increasingly required to provide verified emissions data to maintain access to the European market. However, existing carbon accounting solutions are predominantly designed for large multinational corporations, making them prohibitively expensive and operationally complex for small and medium-sized exporters. This market gap creates an opportunity for a localized, affordable, and intelligent compliance platform tailored to the needs of Southeast Asian manufacturers.

**Emitra** is an AI-powered Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform that automates carbon compliance and export intelligence for Indonesian exporters. The platform streamlines the CBAM reporting process by utilizing artificial intelligence to extract production and emissions-related data from operational documents, calculate embedded emissions based on standardized methodologies, and generate compliance-ready reports with complete audit trails. Unlike global enterprise platforms, Emitra is specifically designed for Southeast Asian exporters, offering an affordable, user-friendly, and scalable solution that minimizes compliance costs while improving reporting accuracy.

Market analysis indicates a highly targeted and rapidly emerging opportunity. Approximately 1,500–2,000 Indonesian exporters are expected to be directly affected by CBAM requirements, representing an estimated export value of approximately USD 757.7 million to the European Union. Emitra will enter this market through strategic partnerships with industrial associations (IISIA, ALFI, KADIN) alongside collaborations with industrial estates. The business adopts a hybrid revenue model: a one-time implementation fee (IDR 30–50 million) plus an annual subscription (IDR 50–80 million/year), positioning Emitra as the first AI-powered carbon compliance platform purpose-built for Southeast Asian exporters.

# 2. PROJECT DESCRIPTION

## 2.1 CBAM EU Regulatory Context

The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) has shifted from a transitional phase (2023–2025) to the **definitive phase as of 1 January 2026** (*European Commission, 2025*). During the transition, exporters were only required to report emission volumes without financial consequences. Starting 2026, a financial phase-in tariff of **2.5%** applies, increasing linearly to **100% full enforcement by 2034** (*European Parliament, 2024*).

Affected upstream commodity sectors include **iron, steel, aluminum, cement, and fertilizers**. Based on European Energy Exchange (EEX) data for 2026, CBAM certificate prices fluctuate around **€75.36 per ton of CO2**. EU importirs must purchase their first CBAM certificates by **February 2027** to cover embedded emissions on products imported during fiscal year 2026.

The impact on Indonesian exporters is indirect but destructive. If exporters cannot provide verified actual emission data, EU importers are forced to use **"Default Values"**—the worst-case emission averages from the highest-emitting producers—resulting in significantly higher certificate costs and eroding the price competitiveness of Indonesian products in the European market.

## 2.2 Indonesia Export Landscape

Based on BPS and Ministry of Trade data, total Indonesian exports to the EU reach **USD 21.5 billion** across all sectors (*BPS, 2025*). Of this, CBAM-affected commodities (iron, steel, aluminum) account for **USD 757.7 million** (*Kementerian Perdagangan RI, 2025*).

The Indonesian Iron and Steel Industry Association (IISIA) reports that steel exports to the EU represent approximately **4% of Indonesia's total global steel exports** (*IISIA, 2025*). Under full enforcement (100% by 2034), the estimated total CBAM certificate burden for the steel supply chain reaches approximately **€383 million**, or **€390 per ton of steel**. However, during the 2026 phase-in period (2.5% tariff), the actual financial exposure is significantly lower—approximately **€9.75 per ton** (total national estimate ~€9.57 million). The **February 2027** certificate purchase deadline creates urgency for exporters to build data infrastructure now, before tariffs escalate exponentially. This regulatory pressure directly impacts **1,500 to 2,000 manufacturing enterprises** across Indonesia's export ecosystem.

## 2.3 Case Study: CV Logam Mulia Sejahtera (Ceper, Klaten)

To understand real-world operational impact, we studied **CV Logam Mulia Sejahtera**, a medium-scale aluminum foundry in Ceper, Klaten, Central Java.

- **Profile:** Manufacturer of cast aluminum with export volumes to Germany and Italy totaling **USD 1.2 million/year**.
- **Market Friction:** Since Q3 2024, EU buyers have required emission data per unit as a prerequisite for contract renewal.
- **Compliance Cost:** Without an integrated internal emission tracking system, the company resortes to third-party environmental consultants for manual audits costing **IDR 150–250 million/year**.
- **Margin Compression:** With net margins of only **8–10%**, audit costs consume up to **14% of annual net profit**—a structural failure for Tier-2/3 manufacturers.

# 5. RECOMMENDED SOLUTION

## 5.1 Solution Overview

Emitra is an AI-powered carbon compliance and export intelligence platform designed to help Indonesian iron, steel, and aluminum exporters produce accurate, verified embedded emissions reports compliant with the EU CBAM declaration format. The platform is not a carbon tax payment tool—it is a data infrastructure enabling exporters to provide verified emission evidence to their EU importers, helping them avoid the Commission's higher default values that erode Indonesian product price competitiveness.

**Design Philosophy:** In compliance regulation, *auditability* matters more than algorithmic sophistication. The architecture combines AI/ML components for manual workload reduction (document extraction, classification, anomaly detection) with a deterministic rule engine following official EU methodology—ensuring every output number can be traced, explained, and defended before external verifiers.

## 5.2 System Architecture

Emitra's architecture consists of five sequential processing layers wrapped by a cross-cutting audit trail and security layer:

**Layer 1 — Input Data:** Accepts four raw document categories: electricity bills (Scope 2), fuel/BBM invoices (Scope 1), production data (export volumes), and bill of materials (raw material composition).

**Layer 2 — OCR & AI Extraction:** Processes documents (scanned PDFs or camera photos) into structured data. Computer vision recognizes tables, numbers, and measurement units across varying document formats.

**Layer 3 — Validation & Normalization:** Unifies structured data from multiple sources into a consistent internal schema while running statistical anomaly checks. Audit trail begins logging data origins.

**Layer 4 — Emission Calculation Engine:** Deterministic engine applying official EU methodology for Scope 1 and Scope 2 embedded emissions. Built as an explicit, auditable rule engine—*not* a black-box model.

**Layer 5 — CBAM XML Report Generator:** Converts calculation results into EU CBAM Registry-compatible declaration format with XSD schema validation before distribution.

## 5.3 AI/ML Pipeline

| Component | Approach | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **OCR Document Parsing** | Document understanding (LayoutLM + character recognition) | Extract values from varied invoice formats |
| **HS Code Auto-Classification** | Text classification model | Map products to CBAM-scope CN codes |
| **Emission Factor Auto-Mapping** | Hybrid: deterministic lookup + light ML | Match energy/fuel activities to emission factors |
| **Anomaly Detection** | Statistical outlier detection (z-score/isolation forest) | Flag unusual consumption spikes for human review |

*Note: The emission calculation engine is intentionally built as a deterministic rule engine—not end-to-end ML—because carbon compliance regulation requires manually traceable methodology, not probabilistic prediction.*

**Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Mechanism:** When OCR Confidence Score falls below 90%, the system does not reject the document. Instead, it displays a side-by-side correction UI—original document photo on the left, input fields on the right—enabling the client's admin staff to confirm or correct extracted values in under 3 seconds. This micro-task approach places the correction burden on the client (who knows their data best), not on Emitra's support team, ensuring scalability without proportionally scaling headcount.

## 5.4 MVP Features

**Scope Limitation:** Year 1 MVP focuses exclusively on **Scope 1** (direct fuel/BBM combustion) and **Scope 2** (electricity consumption from PLN). **Scope 3** (supply chain emissions from raw material suppliers) is excluded from the MVP due to the complexity of tracking upstream supplier data in Indonesia—Scope 3 capabilities are deferred to the Year 3 roadmap.

- **OCR Invoice & BOM Upload** — PDF/image upload, automatic extraction with manual correction interface.
- **Scope 1 & 2 Emission Calculator** — EU methodology, supports default values and verified actual data.
- **CBAM Declarant Report Generator** — XML output aligned with CBAM Registry format.
- **Audit-Trail Log** — Every number clickable to source document and processing stage.

# 8. RISK ANALYSIS

## 8.1 Technical Risks

**Data Quality:** Inconsistent input formats (varying invoice formats, poor photos, manual records) can cascade into incorrect calculations. *Mitigation:* Standardized input templates + AI validation flagging values outside reasonable ranges for human review.

**Regulation Evolution:** CBAM is still actively evolving—additional implementing actions published since late 2025, phase-in rising from 2.5% (2026) to 100% (2034), certificate pricing shifting from quarterly to weekly in 2027. *Mitigation:* Modular calculation engine with configurable parameters (emission factors, phase-in rates, XML schemas) updateable without core logic changes.

**Verification Rejection:** EU-side accredited verifiers may reject Emitra's methodology or data. *Mitigation:* Dual internal verification (automated + manual) before client delivery + partnership with accredited local/regional verification bodies.

**Adoption Skepticism:** Target SME owners are conservative and skeptical of "AI" claims on compliance tools affecting finances directly. *Mitigation:* Transparent audit trail + human-in-the-loop positioning—the system assists, never replaces, client finance/compliance teams.

**Dirty Data Reality:** Factory documents in Indonesia (truck weigh bills, fuel receipts) are often handwritten, wet, torn, or printed on fading thermal paper. If OCR fails to read 30% of data, efficiency claims weaken. *Mitigation:* HITL correction UI (side-by-side view) ensures micro-task correction by client staff, not Emitra support. API integration with accounting software (Jurnal.id, Accurate) planned for Year 2 to reduce OCR dependency.

**Legal Liability:** If Emitra miscalculates and an exporter faces EU penalties, who bears the financial risk? *Mitigation:* Emitra's ToS explicitly limits liability to subscription fees paid. Emitra is a software tool, not an accredited verifier. Final verification responsibility rests with independent bodies (Sucofindo/SGS) that stamp reports before CBAM submission.

**Data Security:** Production and energy data are competitively sensitive. *Mitigation:* End-to-end encryption, logical tenant isolation, RBAC, ISO 27001 certification targeted in Year 2.

## 8.2 Operational Risks

**Cloud Dependency:** Single provider downtime disrupts client access. *Mitigation:* Multi-region architecture (Singapore primary, Jakarta DR) with automatic failover.

**Regulatory Sudden Change:** EU updates formulas or reporting formats. *Mitigation:* Regulatory Monitoring Task Force + technical sprint response within 14 business days.

## 8.3 Business Risks

**Slow Adoption:** *Mitigation:* Free 3-month pilot program + website ROI calculator.

**Bank Partnership Dependency (KUR Hijau):** *Mitigation:* Diversified channels—direct sales, industry associations, B2B marketplaces.

**Global Competition:** *Mitigation:* Local moat through RI-EU regulatory understanding, affordable pricing, local workflow integration, continuous CBAM regulation updates.
